Why Cardinal exists

Bible apps have become social platforms, full of feeds and features that distract you from simply reading the Bible.

Cardinal is different. It was built for one person, alone with Scripture, for as long as they want to stay there. Everything in it is judged by whether it helps you read, or pulls you away from reading.

That is why so much is missing on purpose.

  • NoFeed, comments, or profilesNothing you do here is performed for anyone.
  • NoStreaks, badges, or missed-day guiltNothing keeps score. Missing a day is not a failure.
  • NoAds, and no tracking you across appsYou are not the product being sold.
  • NoGate on readingThe Bible itself never sits behind a subscription.

Read

Plain modern English, from the first tap.

Cardinal opens on the Berean Standard Bible. No setup, no account, no choosing a plan before you are allowed to read a verse.

  • Seventeen translations. Eight of them live on your phone and read offline, including the King James, Berean Standard, World English and Majority Standard Bibles.
  • Highlight the exact words. Long-press and drag across a phrase and only those words are marked, not the whole verse around them. Where the phrase runs past a line break the mark stays one continuous stroke instead of stacked rectangles, rounded at every corner and set on the letters rather than floating above them.
  • Search that respects the wording. Every translation carries its own index, because the words they choose are not the same.
  • Favorites and margins. Save a verse, or write a note that stays with the passage and is searchable in Bible order.

What a tap opens.

The study tools are not in a separate mode you have to go and find. Tap a word, a verse, or the chapter title, and they are already there. Three of the four need no connection at all.

Ask

“What does that actually mean?”

Everyone hits a verse they do not understand. Ask, and get an answer drawn from the passage in front of you: cross-references, word studies, historical context.

  • Thirty questions, answered in full, free. Why is there suffering. Did Jesus claim to be God. What happens when we die. No subscription, nothing to unlock.
  • Three of your own each month. Free, and they come back every month rather than running out for good.
  • Honest about uncertainty. “This passage is often interpreted as” rather than “this means.” Every answer shows the verses it rests on, so you can read the text yourself.
  • Your licensed translations stay private. Publisher-licensed text is never sent to the model. It reads a public-domain rendering, and only the name of your translation travels.

Quiet Time

One sitting a day, about fifteen minutes.

You are never handed a chapter and left to it. Each day is a curated passage of fifteen to twenty-five verses, the natural scene the text is built around.

  • Six steps. Pause, read, reflect, apply, pray, close. The same shape every day, so you stop having to decide how to begin.
  • Nine curated paths. From the Story of Jesus in John, to thirty days in the Psalms, to the whole Bible in a year.
  • An evening sitting. After seven in the evening a shorter one appears to close the day. It never moves your place in the path.
  • A prayer list that remembers. Add what you prayed for at the close, and mark it answered when God shows up.
  • New to reading on your own? Start here. The whole thing is designed for someone who has never done this alone before.

Your archive is searchable, and it is private. Nothing keeps score.

Memorize

Learn it by heart, without a scoreboard.

Two ways to practice, and a deck that quietly works out which verses you are still learning.

  • First letters. The shape of the verse stays on screen. The words go.
  • Hidden words. Blanks out more of the verse on each pass, until you can say it from the reference alone.
  • It asks how that went. Verses you are still learning come back sooner, waiting under “Ready to come back” rather than as a streak you broke.
  • More than fifty curated packs. Grouped by what you are carrying: grief, anxiety, wisdom, thanksgiving. Or build a pack from your own verses.

And the rest of it.

Everything below is included for everyone, with no subscription, unless it says otherwise.

Listen

Scripture read aloud, with the words lighting up in time with the narration and the page following on its own. Six narrators, a sleep timer, adjustable pace, AirPlay, and it keeps playing on the lock screen.

Journeys

Seventeen journeys flown place to place over real 3D satellite terrain. Paul's voyages, Abraham's road, the Exodus, Passion Week. Read each stop's story, hear it narrated, share it as a card of the place itself.

Sermon notes

Structured the way sermons are actually preached: big idea, main points with their own verses, application, ways to serve. Group them by series. For sermons you hear and sermons you give.

Studies

A workspace for preparing one. Gather verses, illustrations and quotes as you read, shape an outline, keep an archive. Editing a study is part of Pro; reading and exporting one never is.

Widgets

A Verse of the Day, today's Quiet Time passage, or your Memorize progress, pinned to the home and lock screen, so the first thing you see in the morning is the Word.

Apple Watch

Four glances: the verse, the next passage, what is playing, and a recall drill. It works cold, with your phone nowhere nearby. The complication is just the verse, not a number telling you how you are doing.

iPad

The same app, laid out for the larger screen. Good for a long sitting at a desk, or for reading two translations side by side.

Works offline

The eight bundled translations read and search with no connection at all. Memorizing, highlighting and note-taking work anywhere. Audio, the streamed translations and Ask need a signal.
